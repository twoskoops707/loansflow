SBA 7(a) vs 504 Loan: Which Fits Your Project?

Short answer: Choose a 7(a) for flexibility, such as working capital, equipment, buying a business or refinancing; choose a 504 for owner-occupied real estate or long-life equipment, where a fixed rate on the CDC portion and a lower down payment can lower long-term cost.
SBA 7(a)SBA 504
Typical useWorking capital, equipment, real estate, buying a business, refinancingOwner-occupied buildings, construction, long-life equipment; not working capital
SizeUp to $5 millionSBA debenture generally up to $5 million; up to $5.5 million for qualifying manufacturers and energy projects (SBA Notice 5000-881796)
StructureOne loan from one lender, SBA guarantees partAbout 50% bank, up to 40% CDC (SBA-backed), 10% or more from you
TermUp to 10 years; up to 25 for real estate10 years (equipment); 20 or 25 years (real estate)
RateNegotiated, capped by SBA, often variable (SBA terms)Fixed on the CDC portion; bank portion set by the lender
SBA fees (FY 2027)2% to 3.75% of the guaranteed portion (Notice 5000-881797)0.50% upfront and 0.203% annual on the debenture

How to decide

  • Need working capital, inventory or to buy a business? Look at the 7(a).
  • Buying or building the building you operate from? Price a 504 too.
  • Run both through the 7(a) calculator and 504 calculator before you talk to a lender.

Common questions

Is a 504 loan cheaper than a 7(a)?

It can be for real estate because of the fixed CDC rate and lower down payment, but fees, rates and your project decide; compare both with a lender.

Can I use a 504 for working capital?

No. A 504 is for fixed assets such as real estate and long-life equipment.
