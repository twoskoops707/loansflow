Calculator Help: Using Every LoansFlow Tool

LoansFlow has nine calculator steps covering SBA and commercial loans, mortgage tools, and live market data. Each section below explains what the step computes, which inputs you need, and when to use it. See the loan glossary for term definitions, and the directory to find lenders.

Step 0 — SBA & Commercial Loan

Open calculator →

SBA & Commercial Loan

What it computes: Monthly payment, total rate, annual debt service, DSCR, and estimated SBA guaranty fees for 7(a), 504, and conventional commercial loans.

Inputs: Loan type, loan amount, amortization years, prime rate, lender spread, SBA guaranty percentage, balloon year (optional), and annual net operating income.

Outputs: Rate (prime plus spread), monthly principal and interest payment, annual debt service, DSCR, balloon balance if applicable, and estimated upfront guaranty fee and annual service fee for SBA loans.

When to use it: To estimate a business loan payment, check whether your cash flow supports the debt, or compare SBA 7(a), 504, and conventional structures.

Step 1 — Commercial Amortization

Open calculator →

Commercial Amortization

What it computes: A year-by-year amortization schedule for your SBA or commercial loan, including a balloon payment row if one applies.

Inputs: The same loan details as Step 0 (loan amount, rate, amortization, balloon year).

Outputs: Yearly principal paid, interest paid, and remaining balance. A chart shows balance over time. You can also export the schedule as a CSV or include it in a PDF report.

When to use it: To see how much principal you will have paid by a certain year, where a balloon payment falls, or how much total interest the loan will generate.

Step 2 — Line of Credit

Open calculator →

Line of Credit

What it computes: A month-by-month projection of draws, interest, payments, and ending balance on a revolving line of credit.

Inputs: Credit limit, projection length in months, prime rate, lender spread, payment type (interest-only or minimum percent of balance), minimum payment percentage, and individual draw or repayment rows with month and amount.

Outputs: Projected balance at the end of the period, total interest paid, total drawn, available credit, and a full month-by-month schedule showing beginning balance, draw or repayment, interest, payment, and ending balance.

When to use it: To model a business line of credit, seasonal draws, or a construction draw schedule. Negative amounts in draw rows represent repayments.

Step 3 — Mortgage: Loan Details

Open calculator →

Mortgage: Loan Details

What it computes: Monthly principal and interest payment, monthly tax, monthly insurance, and total monthly PITI payment.

Inputs: Home price, down payment, interest rate, loan term (10, 15, 20, or 30 years), annual property tax rate (percentage of home price), and annual homeowners insurance.

Outputs: Loan amount, monthly P&I, monthly tax, monthly insurance, total monthly payment, and lifetime interest estimate.

When to use it: When entering or refining core numbers for a mortgage scenario before moving to the breakdown, amortization, refinance, or affordability steps.

Step 4 — Mortgage: Payment Breakdown

Open calculator →

Mortgage: Payment Breakdown

What it computes: A visual breakdown of the total monthly mortgage payment into principal and interest, property tax, and homeowners insurance.

Inputs: The same mortgage inputs from Step 3 (home price, down payment, rate, term, tax rate, insurance).

Outputs: Estimated monthly payment, payment composition by percentage, line-item totals for each component, loan amount, and total interest over the loan term. An interactive chart shows where each dollar goes.

When to use it: To see how much of your monthly payment builds equity versus covers taxes and insurance, or to compare different rate or term combinations.

Step 5 — Mortgage: Amortization Ledger

Open calculator →

Mortgage: Amortization Ledger

What it computes: A year-by-year and month-by-month amortization schedule, with optional extra monthly and one-time balloon payments.

Inputs: Extra monthly payment amount, balloon payment amount, and the month number in which the balloon applies.

Outputs: Yearly principal paid, interest paid, and remaining balance. The tool reports interest saved and months saved if extra or balloon payments shorten the schedule. You can view every payment or export to CSV or PDF.

When to use it: To understand how quickly you will pay off the loan, how much equity you will have in a given year, or how an extra payment strategy changes the timeline.

Step 6 — Mortgage: Refinance Comparison

Open calculator →

Mortgage: Refinance Comparison

What it computes: A side-by-side comparison of your current loan versus a refinanced loan at a new rate and term.

Inputs: New interest rate and new loan term (30, 20, or 15 years). Current rate and term come from the mortgage steps.

Outputs: Current monthly P&I, new monthly P&I, estimated monthly savings or cost increase, and lifetime interest on each loan.

When to use it: When deciding whether to refinance. The comparison assumes the same loan amount and does not include closing costs, points, or rate-lock fees.

Step 7 — Mortgage: Affordability

Open calculator →

Mortgage: Affordability

What it computes: An estimate of how much home you can afford based on your income, existing debts, down payment, and an assumed interest rate.

Inputs: Annual household income, other monthly debt payments, available down payment, and assumed interest rate.

Outputs: Maximum home price estimate, estimated P&I budget, and estimated debt-to-income ratio.

When to use it: When shopping for a home and want a quick price-range sense before talking to a lender. The tool uses 28% and 36% DTI guidelines as planning figures, not lender requirements. It assumes a 30-year term and does not subtract tax or insurance from your budget.

Step 8 — Market News & Rates

Open calculator →

Market News & Rates

What it computes: A live snapshot of national mortgage rate averages and recent news headlines in home lending and small business financing.

Inputs: None — this step pulls from a live feed.

Outputs: The Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey 30-year fixed, 15-year fixed, and 5/1 ARM averages, the prime rate, and up to ten recent news articles.

When to use it: To see current rate averages before entering a mortgage or SBA scenario. Values are weekly survey averages, not personalized quotes, and the feed refreshes automatically while the tab is open.

How the Steps Connect

Each mortgage step builds on the previous one. Start with Step 3 to enter home price, down payment, rate, and term. Step 4 shows how that payment breaks down. Step 5 adds the amortization schedule. Step 6 compares a refinance rate and term. Step 7 turns your income and debts into a price range.

For business financing, Step 0 is the starting point. Step 1 expands it into a full amortization ledger, and Step 2 models a separate revolving line of credit. Step 8 sits at the end of both flows and gives you live rate context.
