DSCR From a CPA’s View

Short answer: DSCR is cash flow available for debt service divided by annual principal and interest payments. Many lenders look for at least 1.25x, but the result depends on which earnings number you use, how owner pay and taxes are treated, and whether the lender tests the business alone or all of your obligations together (global DSCR).

The general formula is on our DSCR explainer. This guide is about what changes the result: the numerator. All figures are illustrations as of November 11, 2026; lender methods differ.

Three common ways to build the numerator

  1. EBITDA basis. Net income plus interest, depreciation and amortization, divided by annual principal and interest. Simple, and generous to pass-through owners because it ignores their personal taxes.
  2. After owner pay. EBITDA minus a market wage for the operator. This is how the DSCR calculator and our acquisition calculator treat acquisitions.
  3. Global. Business cash flow plus the owners’ other income, minus personal taxes and living expenses, divided by business debt service plus personal debt payments.

Example 1: the same business, several answers

Use the business from the add-backs guide and the acquisition starting case, where annual debt service is $120,921.

The business did not change; the lens did. SDE flatters the result because the new owner must be paid, which is why lenders subtract a market salary and why unsupported add-backs matter. A lender that stresses earnings is asking how much cushion remains if results slip.

Example 2: global DSCR

Consider a different, smaller owner-operated business. Suppose its cash flow after the operator’s salary is $180,000 and a spouse earns $40,000. Personal income taxes and living expenses are $70,000. Business debt service is $120,921 and personal debts (mortgage and a car) are $30,000 a year.

Business DSCR: $180,000 ÷ $120,921 = 1.49x.
Global DSCR: ($180,000 + $40,000 − $70,000) ÷ ($120,921 + $30,000) = $150,000 ÷ $150,921 = 0.99x.

A business that passes alone can fail when the owner’s household is counted. Lenders that use a global test usually ask for personal tax returns, a personal financial statement and a debt schedule, so gather them before you apply (see the loan document checklist).

Owner pay, taxes and pass-through entities

  • For S corporations, reasonable compensation must be paid before distributions; a low-salary, high-distribution structure overstates coverage once a lender normalizes pay.
  • Partnership and LLC owners pay tax personally on allocated income whether or not it is distributed, which a global test reflects.
  • C corporations pay entity-level tax, so the numerator is commonly after-tax earnings plus non-cash items.

What lenders also adjust

  • Debt service: they use the new loan’s payment plus existing obligations that remain, including equipment leases and owner loans; the $350,000 SBA example has an annual payment of about $55,503 on its own.
  • Seasonality: a strong year can hide weak quarters; lenders may review monthly results.
  • Capital expenditures: maintenance spending is real cash even when depreciation is non-cash.
  • Fees: the SBA guarantee fee is financed or paid at closing; see the tax treatment guide.

Common mistakes

  • Using EBITDA while ignoring taxes an owner must pay on pass-through income
  • Forgetting existing debt payments, including owner loans and leases
  • Using projections without support
  • Ignoring the minimum a specific lender uses; there is no single SBA-wide DSCR, so ask the lender how it calculates it

How to improve DSCR before you apply

  1. Lengthen the term, which lowers the payment; compare terms in the loan payment calculator.
  2. Increase the down payment (equity injection calculator).
  3. Pay down or refinance small, high-payment debts first.
  4. Document legitimate add-backs early.
  5. Choose the loan structure deliberately; compare 7(a) and 504.

General information, not tax or financial advice. Lender minimums and methods differ.

Common questions

What DSCR do lenders want?

Many look for at least 1.25x, but each lender sets its own minimum and method, so ask how yours calculates it.

What is global DSCR?

A coverage ratio that combines business cash flow with the owners’ other income and subtracts personal taxes and living costs, then divides by business and personal debt payments.

Does DSCR use EBITDA or net income?

It depends on the lender. Many start from EBITDA, then subtract owner compensation and, for pass-through entities, sometimes owner taxes.

How can I improve my DSCR?

Lengthen the term, increase your down payment, reduce other debt payments and document legitimate add-backs.
