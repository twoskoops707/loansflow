About LoansFlow

LoansFlow is an independent, educational website for people researching small business and home financing. It is built for borrowers who want to understand loan options, estimate costs, and prepare better questions before speaking with a lender, SBA resource partner, housing counselor, accountant, or attorney.

LoansFlow is not a lender, broker, bank, marketplace that takes applications, or financial/legal/tax advisor. We do not issue approvals, quotes, pre-approvals, or credit decisions. Our tools and guides are designed to make complex financing topics easier to compare and verify.

Who writes LoansFlow

Founder and author, LoansFlow

Joshua Miller, CPA

Small business financing, valuations, acquisitions and succession, from a CPA’s point of view.

  • Licensed CPA, California (2009)
  • B.S., Cal Poly Humboldt
  • Nearly two decades in public accounting

For nearly two decades Joshua has helped businesses and individuals secure loans, from private lenders to SBA loans and everywhere in between, most often to fund an acquisition. He works with owner-operated and closely held businesses on the money decisions that matter most: how to finance growth, how to buy or sell a company, what a business is worth, how it passes to the next owner, and what it all means for the tax return and the cash flow behind it. He holds a Bachelor of Science from Cal Poly Humboldt (formerly Humboldt State University) and founded LoansFlow to put that practical, numbers-first view in front of borrowers before they sit down with a lender.

What he helps with

Financing and cash flow

Helping businesses and individuals obtain loans for acquisitions and growth, from private lenders to SBA. Lenders approve loans on cash flow. Clean statements, a clear debt schedule and an honest coverage ratio, prepared the way a lender will read them.

Valuations

A defensible earnings figure, adjusted for owner pay, personal expenses and one-time items, with every adjustment documented.

Acquisitions

Equity, seller notes, price allocation and cash at closing: the questions a standard loan never raises.

Succession and life events

Retirement, a partner’s exit, a family transition, or a death, disability, divorce or estate plan that forces a decision about the business.

Tax planning for owners

S corporations, C corporations, partnerships and nonprofits; multi-state businesses; research credits; and how structure changes the result of a loan or a deal.

Management and advisory

Temporary and interim CFO work, negotiations, and permitting work, alongside financial statements and cash flow worksheets.

What you can do on LoansFlow

Compare loan types. Each major loan page explains what the loan is used for, who it may fit, typical requirements, tradeoffs, and how the application process usually works. Coverage includes SBA 7(a), SBA 504, SBA Express, SBA Microloans, business lines of credit, commercial term loans, equipment financing, and mortgages.

Run planning calculators. Free calculators estimate payments, SBA fee impacts, debt service coverage (DSCR), affordability ranges, line-of-credit costs, equipment payments, refinance break-even points, and related scenarios. Calculations run in your browser. They are estimates only, not offers or lender commitments. See How Our Numbers Work for formulas and assumptions.

Use practical guides. Step-by-step guides cover application preparation, lender selection, document checklists, SBA eligibility concepts, DSCR, closing costs, and common decision points. Guides are written to help readers organize information and ask better questions; they do not replace advice tailored to a specific situation.

Research funding resources. LoansFlow includes a live Grants.gov search, a page on alternative financing options (including scam warnings), a directory of SBA offices/resource partners and selected lender/marketplace listings, and a news and rates page that links to original publishers and public data sources.

How we create and maintain content

We aim to make factual claims easy to verify. Program rules, fee schedules, forms, limits, and office details are checked against primary sources where practical, such as SBA.gov, CFPB, IRS, HUD, Federal Reserve/Freddie Mac data releases, and Grants.gov. Pages that rely on external programs may be updated when rules, rates, or source material changes.

Our full process is described in the Editorial Policy and Review Standards. That page explains sourcing, calculator assumptions, corrections, and how advertising/sponsored links are handled.

Independence and transparency

LoansFlow is independently operated. The site may display advertising, including Google AdSense, and may include clearly labeled partner or informational links. Advertising does not change calculator math. Marketplace and directory listings are informational, not endorsements, and readers should verify current terms directly with the provider or agency.

We do not ask users to submit loan applications through LoansFlow, and calculator inputs are not used to make lending decisions on this site. For details about data and advertising technologies, see our Privacy Policy.

Corrections

If you find an error, outdated information, broken source, or unclear explanation, please contact us with the page URL and the issue. Factual corrections are prioritized, especially those involving program rules, fees, forms, calculator behavior, and source links.
