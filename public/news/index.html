How to read rate news as a borrower

Reviewed by Joshua Miller, CPA · updated November 13, 2026

Prime rate. Many business lines of credit and variable-rate SBA 7(a) loans are priced as the prime rate plus a spread, so when prime changes, those payments can change on the lender’s reset date. The SBA caps the spread by loan size; see SBA 7(a) maximum rates. Prime is published by the Federal Reserve and tracked by FRED (DPRIME).

Mortgage averages. The weekly 30-year and 15-year figures come from the Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey. They are national averages, not quotes; your rate depends on credit, down payment, points and lender.

What to do with a headline. A Federal Reserve decision matters mostly to variable-rate borrowers. If you have a fixed-rate loan, a rate cut helps only if you refinance, and refinancing has costs: use the refinance break-even calculator. If you are about to borrow, re-run your numbers in the SBA 7(a) calculator with today’s prime.

Why this matters when you borrow

Rates on mortgages follow long-term Treasury yields. Rates on many business loans and lines of credit follow the prime rate, which moves with Federal Reserve decisions. Rules from the CFPB change what lenders must disclose. Reading the news will not time the market for you, but it helps you understand why a quote changed.
