Loan type

Business Line of Credit

A revolving credit limit you draw on, repay, and draw on again.

How it works
Draw as needed up to a limit; interest only on the balance
Typical use
Payroll gaps, inventory, seasonal swings
Rate
Usually variable, tied to prime
SBA version
CAPLines: up to $5 million, maximum 10-year maturity (Builders lines up to 60 months plus construction)
Costs to watch
Annual or draw fees, unused-line fees, renewal fees
Best for
Uneven cash flow, not long-term assets

What it is

A line of credit works like a business credit card with a much higher limit and a lower rate. You are approved for a maximum, borrow what you need when you need it, and pay interest only on what is outstanding. As you repay, the room opens up again.

That makes it ideal for timing gaps: waiting on a customer payment, buying inventory ahead of a busy season, or covering a slow month. It is a poor fit for buying equipment or real estate, because lines are shorter-term and usually renewed periodically.

Never spend your money before you have it. Thomas Jefferson, Letter to Thomas Jefferson Smith, February 21, 1825Documented

The plain-English version of "do not borrow against income you only hope to earn."

A good fit if you want

  • Cash-flow gaps and seasonal businesses
  • Having a safety net without paying interest on idle money
  • Unpredictable amounts

Probably not the right tool if

  • Financing a long-lived asset
  • Borrowers who tend to leave the balance near the limit, which can lead lenders to cut the line

Typical requirements

  • Business bank statements and financials
  • Often a personal guarantee
  • Collateral such as receivables, inventory or a blanket lien
  • SBA CAPLines follow the same eligibility as a 7(a)

How to apply

  1. Know your cycleMap when cash goes out and comes in over the year to size the limit.
  2. Compare lendersBanks usually price lower; online lenders approve faster. Compare the all-in cost.
  3. Gather documentsBank statements, tax returns, financial statements and an accounts receivable report if you have one.
  4. Apply and negotiateAsk about draw fees, renewal terms and whether the rate is capped.
  5. Use it, then repay itTreat the line as a bridge, not permanent funding.

Common questions

What is the difference between a term loan and a line of credit?

A term loan gives you the full amount up front and charges interest on all of it. A line lets you borrow in pieces and pay interest only on what you use.

Can the lender cut my line?

Yes. Lines are commonly reviewed and renewed, and lenders can reduce or freeze them if your finances weaken.
