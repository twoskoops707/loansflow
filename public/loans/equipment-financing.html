Loan type

Equipment Financing

Loans and leases for machinery, vehicles and technology, secured by the equipment itself.

Collateral
The equipment itself (usually a lien filed under the UCC)
Term
Matched to useful life: about 3 years for IT to 7 years for heavy equipment
Down payment
Often 0% to 20%
Rate
Roughly 6% for strong credit to 30%+ for weak credit; see the table below
Speed
Often days for smaller deals
Best for
Machinery, trucks, medical, restaurant and tech equipment

What it is

Equipment financing lets you buy machinery, vehicles or technology and pay for it over time, with the equipment as the collateral. Because the lender can repossess and resell the asset, equipment loans are often easier to qualify for than unsecured loans, and approval can be quick. What it costs depends on three things: your credit, what you are buying, and how long you borrow.

Credit sets the starting rate. The equipment type adjusts it: construction and manufacturing equipment hold their value and get the best pricing, while computers and office furniture lose value quickly, so lenders charge more and shorten the term. Used equipment and small down payments also raise the rate.

Penny wise and pound foolish. Robert Burton, The Anatomy of Melancholy (1621)Documented

Saving a little on the rate while ignoring fees, prepayment penalties or a bad term is exactly this mistake.

A good fit if you want

  • Buying a specific machine, vehicle or system
  • Preserving cash and credit lines for operations
  • Matching the payment term to the equipment's useful life

Probably not the right tool if

  • Working capital or general expenses
  • Buying equipment that will be obsolete before the loan ends

Typical requirements

  • An invoice or quote for the equipment
  • Time-in-business and bank statements; startups may need a larger down payment
  • Credit review of the business and owners
  • Proof of insurance on the equipment

How to apply

  1. Get a quoteA written quote from the vendor tells the lender exactly what is being financed.
  2. Decide: loan or leaseA loan builds ownership. A lease may lower payments and can include an end-of-term buyout; compare total cost.
  3. Check your creditReview your personal and business credit reports. Fix errors before applying.
  4. Compare offersGet offers from your bank, the equipment vendor's financing arm and an online lender. Compare APR and total cost.
  5. Close and take deliveryThe lender usually pays the vendor directly and files a lien on the equipment.

Need a lender, an SBA district office or a free counselor? Use the national directory.

Common questions

Loan or lease?

With a loan you own the equipment and can depreciate it. A lease usually has lower payments and may offer a fair-market-value or fixed buyout at the end. Which is cheaper depends on the buyout and your tax situation.

Is there a tax benefit?

The tax code lets many businesses deduct the cost of qualifying equipment in the year it is placed in service, subject to annual limits that change. Ask your tax professional what applies to you.

Can the SBA finance equipment?

Yes. 7(a) loans and 504 loans (for long-life equipment) can both finance equipment, often with longer terms.
