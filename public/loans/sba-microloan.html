SBA Microloan

Small loans up to $50,000 from nonprofit lenders, with help for newer businesses.

What it is

Microloans are the SBA's smallest product and work differently from every other one. The SBA lends money to nonprofit community organizations called intermediaries, and the intermediaries lend up to $50,000 to small businesses and startups. Many also provide training and mentoring.

Rates are higher than a bank's SBA loan, but intermediaries are more willing to work with startups, owners with thin credit, and businesses banks turn away.

Beware of little expenses; a small leak will sink a great ship. Benjamin Franklin, Poor Richard's AlmanackDocumented

Small recurring fees add up. It is why lenders and borrowers should both read the fee line by line, not just the rate.

A good fit if you want

  • Startups and very small businesses
  • Owners rebuilding credit
  • Borrowers who want business coaching along with the loan

Probably not the right tool if

  • Real estate purchases or refinancing
  • Anyone who needs more than $50,000

Typical requirements

  • Eligible for-profit small business (some nonprofit childcare centers also qualify)
  • A business plan and cash-flow projections
  • Intermediary-specific credit and collateral standards, often more flexible than a bank's

How to apply

  1. Find an intermediaryUse the SBA's microlender search or our directory to find one that serves your area.
  2. Attend any required counselingSome intermediaries require training before or after funding.
  3. Apply with a planBring a short business plan, projections and personal financial information.
  4. Receive fundsClosing is generally simpler and faster than a bank SBA loan.

Need a lender, an SBA district office or a free counselor? Use the national directory.

Common questions

Is there a minimum?

Intermediaries set their own minimums; amounts range from a few hundred dollars up to $50,000.

Can I use it to buy a building?

No. Real estate and refinancing existing debt are not allowed.
