Terms used in this calculator

SBA guarantee
The share of the loan the SBA promises to repay the lender if you default: 85% on standard 7(a) loans up to $150,000 and 75% above that (Express loans are 50%). You still owe the whole loan.
Fee waiver
The SBA waives the upfront fee on qualifying 7(a) loans of $700,000 or less for certain borrowers, such as manufacturers, listed food-supply-chain businesses and rural businesses. Ask your lender whether you qualify.
Upfront guarantee fee
A one-time fee charged on the SBA-guaranteed part of the loan. The lender pays it to the SBA and usually passes it to you. Rolling it into the loan avoids cash at closing, but you pay interest on it.

How to use it

Enter the loan amount and the SBA guarantee percentage. The standard 7(a) guarantee is already filled in for the loan size you enter; change it for Express loans. Choose whether you qualify for the fee waiver and whether you would roll the fee into the loan or pay it in cash at closing.

How the fee is calculated

The SBA charges the upfront fee on the guaranteed portion of the loan, not on the whole loan. The tier is chosen by the gross loan amount: 2% of the guaranteed portion for loans up to $150,000, 3% of the guaranteed portion for loans from $150,001 to $700,000, and above $700,000 it is 3.50% of the guaranteed portion up to $1,000,000 plus 3.75% of any guaranteed amount above that. The lender separately pays a 0.55% annual service fee on the guaranteed balance and may not charge it to you. Rules are the SBA's FY 2027 fee notice, effective 2026-10-01.

Worked example

A $350,000 loan with the standard 75% guarantee has a guaranteed portion of $262,500. In the 3% tier the fee is $7,875, or 2.25% of the loan. If you roll it in, the balance becomes $357,875. A $1,200,000 loan at 75% has a guaranteed portion of $900,000, so the fee is $31,500, or 2.63% of the loan.

How to read the result

The fee is a real cost even when a lender pays it first and passes it to you. Compare it with the interest saved by choosing a longer term or a lower rate, and ask your lender whether the fee will be financed. If you are a manufacturer, a listed food-supply-chain business or a rural business borrowing $700,000 or less, the fee may be waived. See the SBA 7(a) page for the full fee table and sources.

Common questions

Who pays the SBA guarantee fee?

The lender pays the upfront fee to the SBA and usually passes it to the borrower. The lender annual service fee cannot be passed on.

Is the fee charged on the whole loan?

No. It is a percentage of the SBA-guaranteed portion, with the percentage set by the loan size.

Can the fee be financed?

Often yes, which avoids cash at closing but means you pay interest on it. Ask your lender.
