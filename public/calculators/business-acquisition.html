Terms used in this calculator

Working capital
Money for day-to-day operations, such as inventory, payroll and receivables, rather than for buying a building or equipment.
Closing costs
Fees and expenses due when the loan closes: lender and packaging fees, title, appraisal, legal and similar items.
Equity injection
Your own cash put into the deal. For an SBA change-of-ownership purchase the minimum is undefined% of the project; your lender may require more.
Seller note
Part of the price the seller finances and you repay to the seller over time. Lenders may require it to be on standby and may limit how it counts toward your equity.
Upfront guarantee fee
A one-time fee charged on the SBA-guaranteed part of the loan. The lender pays it to the SBA and usually passes it to you. Rolling it into the loan avoids cash at closing, but you pay interest on it.
SDE (seller discretionary earnings)
Net profit plus the owner’s salary and perks, interest, depreciation and amortization, and documented one-time costs. It shows what one owner-operator earns from the business before paying themselves. Small business prices are usually quoted as a multiple of SDE.
Market salary for the operator
What it would cost to hire someone to run the business in your place. Lenders subtract it from the earnings so the business still covers the loan after paying a fair wage.

How to use it

Enter the purchase price, any working capital you want to borrow and your expected closing costs. Set your equity injection (the SBA minimum for a change of ownership is 10% of the project) and, if the seller will carry a note, its size. Then enter the business’s seller’s discretionary earnings (SDE) and the market salary you would pay yourself as the operator. The result shows your cash at closing, the SBA loan and payment, and the debt service coverage ratio (DSCR).

How it is calculated

Total uses are price plus working capital plus closing costs. Equity is your percentage of total uses. The SBA loan is what remains after equity and any seller note, plus the SBA guarantee fee if you choose to finance it. The fee is a percentage of the SBA-guaranteed portion (75% on loans over $150,000) and the tier is set by the gross loan amount, so financing the fee can move a loan into the next tier. Cash flow available for debt service is SDE minus the owner’s market salary; DSCR is that figure divided by annual debt service (SBA loan plus seller note). A seller note, if you enter one, is modeled at 6% over 10 years. SBA lenders must follow SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026), which limits how a seller note counts toward your equity, so treat that line as a planning assumption and ask your lender.

Worked example

A $750,000 purchase with $50,000 of working capital and $25,000 of costs is $825,000 of uses. At 10% equity you bring $82,500. The SBA loan is about $742,500 before the fee; at 3.5% of the guaranteed portion the fee is about $20,016, and financing it brings the loan to about $762,516. At 10.00% over 10 years the payment is about $10,077 a month ($120,920 a year). With SDE of $260,000 and an $80,000 owner salary, cash flow is $180,000 and DSCR is about 1.49x. Those are the calculator’s starting values, so you can reproduce them above. Rate shown: prime 7.00% (FRED DPRIME, November 6, 2026) plus 3.00%; your rate will differ.

What lenders also look at

DSCR is only one test. Lenders review the business valuation and the add-backs behind SDE, collateral, your industry experience and your personal credit. A CPA’s view of how earnings were normalized matters; see owner add-backs and EBITDA normalization and DSCR from a CPA’s view.

Common questions

How much down payment do I need to buy a business with an SBA loan?

The SBA requires at least 10% equity injection on a change of ownership, and your lender may require more. Seller notes can count toward the injection only under specific SBA rules, so confirm with your lender.

What DSCR do SBA lenders look for?

Many lenders look for at least 1.25x, but each lender sets its own minimum.

Can I finance the SBA guarantee fee?

Often yes; the fee can be added to the loan. That raises the loan amount and payment slightly.
