Open tools

How our numbers work

Every figure these calculators produce comes from a formula printed below. You can check the arithmetic yourself instead of trusting us. Everything runs in your browser, and every input is yours to change.

These are estimates for planning. They are not a loan offer, a pre-approval, a rate quote, or an APR, and they do not include lender fees or closing costs unless a section says so.

One note before you start: each field opens holding a sample value so the form is not empty. Those starting values are placeholders, not market rates. Replace them with figures from a lender before you rely on any result.

The math we use

Monthly payment

One formula produces the payment in every level-payment tool on this site — SBA, commercial, mortgage, refinance, and affordability. The line of credit runs on a different loop, described further down.

monthly payment = P × r(1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)
r = annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12
n = term in years × 12
if r is 0, the payment is simply P ÷ n

What it needs: the amount borrowed, an annual rate as a percentage, and a term in years.

What it leaves out: fees, points, closing costs, and any APR calculation. The rate is used as a flat fixed rate for the whole term. Adjustable-rate loans are not modelled.

Amortization schedules

The monthly payment above is only the headline. The ledgers walk the loan month by month using this loop, once for each loan in the tool:

  • Interest for the month = opening balance × r.
  • Scheduled principal = base monthly payment − that month's interest.
  • Any extra principal you enter is added on top, every month.
  • A one-time balloon, if you set one, is added in the month number you choose.
  • Principal paid in a month is capped at the remaining balance, so the schedule never goes below zero.
  • The schedule stops early once the balance is paid off.

User-editable: the mortgage ledger adds an extra monthly payment, a balloon amount, and the month that balloon applies in.

What it leaves out: rate changes, fees, and prepayment penalties.

You can view the same schedule as an annual summary, a quarterly summary, every single payment, or 30 days of daily interest. Daily interest is simple interest on the opening balance for the next 30 days: balance × rate ÷ day count. The day count is 365 for mortgages and 360 for commercial and SBA loans. The year-by-year CSV exports and the PDF report are generated from the same schedule rather than retyped.

In the mortgage ledger, when you add an extra or balloon payment the tool runs the same schedule a second time with none of them, then reports the difference as interest saved and months saved.

SBA 7(a), SBA 504, and conventional commercial loans

rate = prime field + spread field
monthly payment = the monthly payment formula above
annual debt service = monthly payment × 12
DSCR = annual net operating income ÷ annual debt service

User-editable: loan type, loan amount, amortization in years, the prime rate field, the lender spread field, the SBA guaranty percentage (0–90), the balloon year, and your annual net operating income.

What it leaves out: origination fees, lender fees, any other expense of the loan, and the interest that would accrue on a floating rate after it resets. The rate is held flat for the whole amortization.

DSCR and the 1.25x figure. The tool marks the result in its top band at 1.25x or above, a middle band between 1.00x and 1.25x, and the bottom band below 1.00x, and picks a caption to match. Those thresholds are display choices inside the calculator, not underwriting rules and not a lender's requirement.

Balloon payments. When you enter a balloon year shorter than the amortization term, the tool runs the full schedule and reports the balance still outstanding at the end of that balloon year. Your monthly payment is not recalculated to retire that balance, and no balloon is shown if the balloon year equals or exceeds the amortization term.

Loan type. The payment and DSCR math is identical for all three types. The guaranty fee lines below appear only when the type is SBA 7(a) or SBA 504; a conventional commercial loan shows none, because no guaranty is involved.

SBA guaranty fee and annual service fee

Both figures are hardcoded schedules inside the tool, not a live lookup. We have deliberately not built a fee table we cannot verify; the numbers below are what the calculator uses, the tool labels them as estimates, and SBA revises these schedules, so confirm the exact figure with an SBA-approved lender.

guaranteed portion = loan amount × guaranty % ÷ 100
fee % comes from the tier below, applied to the guaranteed portion
guaranty fee = guaranteed portion × fee %
annual service fee = loan amount × guaranty % ÷ 100 × 0.55%

  • Guaranteed portion up to $150,000: fee is 2.0%.
  • Up to $700,000: 3.0%.
  • Up to $1,000,000: 3.5%.
  • Above $1,000,000: 3.75%.

What this leaves out: the tiers in the tool cover the standard schedule only. It does not model the reduced or waived fees some programs allow for small loans, or the separate schedules that apply to manufacturer or veteran loans, and SBA revises its schedule, so confirm the exact figure with an SBA-approved lender.

Commercial amortization ledger

The commercial ledger reuses the amortization loop and the monthly payment formula above, then groups the schedule into years: principal paid and interest paid are the sums of that year's months, and the remaining balance is the last row of the year. Total interest over the term is the sum of every month's interest. Its daily interest view uses a 360-day year. You can export the year rows as CSV or take them into the PDF report.

Line of credit

This one is a revolving projection rather than a level-payment loan, so it has its own loop. Each month the tool adds up every draw you entered for that month, where a negative amount is a repayment, and then:

  • Balance after the month's draws = opening balance + net draws.
  • Interest = balance after draws × annual rate ÷ 100 ÷ 12. A draw taken in month 1 therefore accrues a full month of interest.
  • Interest-only payment: the payment equals the interest and no principal is repaid.
  • Minimum percent of balance: the payment is the larger of the interest and your minimum percentage of the balance. Principal repaid = payment − interest, capped at the balance.
  • The balance never goes below zero.

User-editable: credit limit, projection length in months, the prime rate field, the spread field, payment type, the minimum payment percentage, and every draw row — you can add and remove rows as needed.

What it reports: the balance at the end of the projection, the total of your positive draws, total interest over the period, and available credit = limit minus the ending balance.

What it leaves out: commitment and facility fees, unused-line fees, over-limit fees, draw fees, and any rate change mid-projection. If a month takes the balance over your limit, the tool warns you but still runs the numbers rather than capping the draw. The CSV export gives you every month of the projection.

Mortgage payment

loan amount = home price − down payment
principal & interest = the monthly payment formula above
monthly property tax = home price × tax rate % ÷ 12
monthly insurance = annual insurance ÷ 12
total monthly payment = P&I + tax + insurance
total interest = P&I × term in months − loan amount

User-editable: home price, down payment, interest rate, term (30, 20, 15, or 10 years), an annual property tax rate entered as a percentage of the home price, and an annual insurance figure.

What it leaves out: mortgage insurance, HOA dues, closing costs, points, prepaid escrow, and utilities. There is no field for any of them, so they are not in the total. Property tax and insurance are treated as flat figures you supply — the tool does not look up an assessment for an address.

The homepage quick estimate uses the same monthly payment formula on price, down payment, rate, and term only. It shows principal and interest, not the full payment.

Refinance comparison

current P&I = the monthly payment formula at your current rate and term
new P&I = the same formula at your new rate and new term
monthly difference = current P&I − new P&I
lifetime interest on each side = P&I × months − loan amount

User-editable: the new rate and the new term (30, 20, or 15 years). The current rate and term come from the mortgage steps.

What it leaves out: closing costs, points, lender fees, rate-lock and extension fees, cash-out, and any change to the balance. The comparison assumes the same loan amount throughout and changes only the rate and the term, so read the monthly saving as interest and principal only.

Affordability

monthly income = annual income ÷ 12
housing guideline = monthly income × 28%
debt guideline = monthly income × 36% − your other monthly debts
P&I budget = the lower of those two
loan amount = the P&I budget run backwards through the monthly payment formula
home price = that loan amount + the down payment you enter
reported DTI = (other monthly debts + P&I budget) ÷ monthly income

User-editable: annual income, other monthly debt payments, the down payment available, and the rate you want to assume.

How it is limited. The 28% and 36% figures are planning guidelines hardcoded in the calculator, not lender rules. This step assumes a 30-year term, and it does not subtract property tax or insurance from your budget — the entire budget goes to principal and interest. It also does not check credit, reserves, or down payment minimums.

Where our reference data comes from

Only two parts of the site pull live reference data: the Market News & Rates step and the rate band on the homepage. Both read one feed, served by our own endpoint at /api/market-news. When that endpoint rebuilds, it collects:

  • The Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey history file (its published PMMS history CSV), for the weekly 30-year fixed, 15-year fixed, and 5/1 ARM averages, plus the change from the previous week's row.
  • The prime rate, from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis series DPRIME. This seeds the prime fields in the SBA and line of credit tools.
  • Federal Reserve monetary policy press releases, up to two items.
  • Google News searches for mortgage rate coverage from the last 7 days, up to 8 items, and for SBA, 7(a), and 504 loan coverage from the last 14 days, up to 4 items.
  • The headline and summary from the Freddie Mac PMMS page, shown as the lead card.

Two things worth knowing about that feed. The "30-Yr Refinance" cell is not a separately published series: our code takes the 30-year fixed average and adds 0.05 percentage points to it, and labels it an approximation against the purchase average. And the news list is filtered before you see it — we cap it at 8 items, drop duplicate headlines, and drop obvious non-US and generic "rates today" headlines.

Values can lag. The PMMS figures are weekly survey averages, not quotes, and the feed can only be as current as its sources. The endpoint is cached at the edge for 30 minutes, the browser re-fetches at most every 30 minutes and stops refreshing while the tab is hidden, and if the feed cannot be reached the tools fall back to a stored snapshot that is regenerated by a daily job on our repository. The rate views show the as-of date of the data behind them.

How those rates reach the forms. Once per page load, the 30-year fixed average is copied into the mortgage and affordability rate fields, the refinance figure into the refinance rate field, and the prime rate into the SBA and line of credit prime fields. That happens only if you have not already changed the field yourself, and not at all if you arrived through a share link — your numbers win either way. None of this is a rate for you. Your rate depends on your credit, your down payment, and the lender, and the calculator is built so you can type in whatever you are actually quoted.

What we don't do

  • No account, no sign-in. There is nothing to register for, and the calculators store nothing about you on a server.
  • No lead forms, no applications. Nothing you type into a calculator is sent to us or to anyone else. The calculators make no network request with your numbers — the only data they fetch is the market news feed described above. Pinned scenarios are saved in your own browser's local storage, and a share link encodes your numbers in the link itself. The PDF report and the CSV files are generated in your browser.
  • Nothing is sold and nothing is submitted. We are not a lender and there is no connection to one. We cannot quote you, pre-approve you, or submit an application, and there is no button anywhere on this site that does. The only form on this site is the contact form, which sends what you choose to write so we can reply to you.
  • No invented rates or fees. Nothing on this site quotes a lender, and every rate and fee is an input you control or a schedule hardcoded in the tool and described above.
  • Advertising and partner links. This page includes Google's AdSense script, and ad units appear in the calculator pages. Our partner list only renders entries that are marked live and carry an approved tracking link; entries still awaiting approval are never shown, and anything that does appear is labelled as sponsored. There are no partner links in this page.
