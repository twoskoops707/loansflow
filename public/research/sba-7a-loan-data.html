SBA 7(a) Loan Data: FY2026 Size, Rates, Industries

General information, not financial, tax or legal advice. LoansFlow is not a lender.

Short answer: In fiscal year 2026, SBA’s public data shows 56,130 7(a) loan approvals totaling $32.4 billion. The median loan was $220,000, the average was $577,703, and 44% of loans were $150,000 or less (data as of September 30, 2026).

Loan volume and size, FY2017–FY2026

SBA 7(a) approvals fell from 78,078 loans in FY2025 to 56,130 in FY2026 (28.1%), while approved dollars fell from $37.3 billion to $32.4 billion (13.0%). Against FY2021, the number of loans rose 8% and dollars fell 11%. Dollar approvals in the data peaked in FY2025 at $37.3 billion.

The median is the better guide for a typical borrower. In FY2026 the average loan was $577,703 but the median was $220,000, so the average was 2.6 times the median: half of all loans were $220,000 or smaller. Loans of $150,000 or less, the range where SBA’s standard guarantee is 85% rather than 75%, were 44% of approvals in FY2026 versus 35% in FY2021. About 13.5% of FY2026 approvals are marked cancelled in the file.

Interest rates and terms

At approval, the median initial interest rate was 9.5% on variable-rate loans and 8% on fixed-rate loans in FY2026, compared with 5.5% and 4.95% in FY2021. Variable rates are priced as a base rate plus a spread, and SBA caps the spread by loan size; the current maximum rates are on our 7(a) rates table.

The median loan term in FY2026 was 10 years (120 months). Terms are set by use of proceeds, and real estate loans run longer than working-capital loans.

Which industries get 7(a) loans

Over FY2022–FY2026, Construction had the most approvals (41,321 loans, 13.4% of the total), and Accommodation and Food Services had the most dollars ($26.8 billion).

How to read this as a borrower

  • Plan around the median, not the average. $220,000 was the middle loan in FY2026. A larger request is not unusual, but it will be underwritten on your own cash flow, not on a national figure. Test yours with the DSCR calculator.
  • The guarantee is the lender’s protection, not yours. SBA guarantees 85% of loans up to $150,000 and 75% above that (FY2027 rules, effective October 1, 2026); you remain responsible for the full loan. See the SBA 7(a) page.
  • Cost is more than the rate. Run the payment and the upfront SBA fee together in the 7(a) calculator.
  • Buying a business? Lenders look at the earnings behind the price. Start with add-backs and EBITDA and the acquisition calculator.

Where the numbers come from

All figures come from the U.S. Small Business Administration’s public 7(a) & 504 FOIA loan data (publisher: SBA; license: U.S. Government Works, public domain). The files used here are dated September 30, 2026. SBA updates the data quarterly, typically about a month after each quarter ends, and these pages are rebuilt from the newest file when it appears. Dollar amounts are nominal (not adjusted for inflation).

  • Loan: Any 7(a) approval record in the file, including loans later cancelled (CANCLD), matching how SBA reports approvals. Cancelled counts are shown separately.
  • Dollars: GrossApproval, the full approved amount in nominal dollars (not adjusted for inflation).
  • Fiscal year: Federal fiscal year, October 1 through September 30, as recorded in ApprovalFY.
  • State: ProjectState, the state where the financed project is located.
  • Industry: First two digits of the NAICS code on the loan record, grouped into NAICS sectors.
  • Medians are shown only for groups with at least 20 loans.

The summary behind these pages is published as one JSON file with no borrower names or individual loan records. Approvals are not a forecast of whether a particular application will be approved, and they are not rate quotes.
